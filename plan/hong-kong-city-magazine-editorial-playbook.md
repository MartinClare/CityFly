# Hong Kong City-Change Magazine — Positioning & Editorial Playbook

> **Version 3.1 — life, tech, open possibilities**  
> Voice lock: Bloomberg CityLab / The Atlantic / Works in Progress. We explain how Hong Kong is changing so readers can see **how new technology might improve a Tuesday**, **what could go wrong**, and **which rumours are interesting but still unproven**. We are independent. Official documents are evidence, not the story. Never sound like we are promoting government policy.

> **Purpose of this document**  
> This is the foundational instruction manual for AI writers, editors, researchers, social-media assistants, and human contributors working on this publication. Read it before researching, outlining, drafting, editing, publishing, or repurposing any story.

---

## 1. Editorial North Star

### What we are

We are a Hong Kong digital magazine that explains how the city is changing — through **property, infrastructure, transport, the economy, technology, investment, business, and Greater Bay Area connections**.

We use serious research and genuine technical understanding, but present every story in a way that is:

- Easy to understand
- Visually engaging
- Conversational and locally relevant
- Interesting enough to share with friends
- Honest about uncertainty, trade-offs, and risk
- Oriented toward what can be built, started, or joined next
- Honest about how technology can help a life, and how it can threaten one

### What we are not

We are not:

- A construction-industry trade journal
- A dry government-policy summary site
- A property-agent lead-generation page
- A stock-tip, property-tip, or financial-advice page
- A generic global-AI news aggregator
- A source of copied press releases
- A government-policy promotion page, even when the source is official
- A rumour mill that treats gossip as fact
- A place for unsupported hype or clickbait

### Core promise

> **用一張圖、一條數、一個現場，講清楚新技術點改你嘅生活——機會、威脅、傳聞，邊樣證實、邊樣仲開住。**

English working version:

> **See how the city — and its tech — might change your life. Keep the rumour open until the fact arrives.**

### The two-layer equation

Every published story must have both layers. Never publish Layer A without Layer B (clickbait). Never publish Layer B without Layer A (homework).

```
Showroom (Layer A)              Engine room (Layer B)
a Tuesday that might change,    mechanism, data, phasing,
opportunity + threat + rumour   constraint, primary sources
```

The research should be rigorous. Official papers sit in the engine room. The showroom is a life, a firm, a construction window, or an unproven possibility — never a press-release paraphrase. The reader should finish thinking 「原來生意、投資觀察、建造都有窗口，亦都要小心呢樣」— not 「又係政府講得好」。

### The three opportunity windows

Every substantial story should map **at least two** of these, and all three when the sources allow. If a layer cannot be supported, say so — do not invent a deal.

| Window | What to show | Never write |
|---|---|---|
| **生意 Business** | A shop, SME, logistics firm, or new service that can start, supply, or adapt | Generic “經濟受惠” |
| **投資觀察 Investment watch** | A land, sector, yield, rate, or listed-name *landscape* worth watching | “你應該買／賣／入市” |
| **建造 Construction** | Who can bid, subcontract, supply materials, or sell professional services during design / tender / build | A contractor advert, or treating a tender notice as a finished project |

Investment language is a **map**, not a tip. Always add the standard disclaimer when this window appears.

## 2. Brand Positioning

### Category

**Hong Kong city-change magazine**

A smart, accessible editorial publication about what is being built, changing, rising, falling, connected, invested in, or disrupted in Hong Kong.

### Reader value

Every substantial story should help a reader answer at least one question:

- What is happening in Hong Kong right now?
- Why does this matter to me, my work, my commute, my neighbourhood, my rent, or my future?
- Which district, industry, or trend is worth watching?
- Is the headline more meaningful than it first appears?
- What is the opportunity — for a **business**, an **investment watch**, or a **construction** participant?
- What new capability, market, or corridor is this creating?
- How could new technology improve a commute, a shop, a flat, or a job?
- What is the threat — privacy, lock-in, job loss, a rumour that might be true?
- What still has to be true for that opening to work?

### Brand personality

The publication should feel:

- **Smart, not academic**
- **Knowledgeable, not arrogant**
- **Local, not parochial**
- **Curious, not cynical**
- **Constructive, not scolding**
- **Opportunity-seeking, not boosterish**
- **Independent, not a government brochure**
- **Slightly provocative, not reckless**
- **Entertaining, not shallow**
- **Visual, not decorative**
- **Professional, not corporate**

### Voice reference

A calm cartographer-engineer who likes builders and distrusts brochures. Notices the new corridor, the new app, the rumour in the group chat, and the data trail it might leave. Lets the fact be the punchline — a useful fact, not a dunk and not a slogan. Coffee conversation is the warmth test. CityLab is the craft test. Works in Progress is the curiosity test.

The reader should think:

> “I finally understand this — and I can see what to do with it.”
> 「原來生活可以咁變，但又有呢個風險。」
> 「呢個傳聞未證實，不過值得盯。」

Not:

> “This feels like homework.”
> “Hong Kong is doomed / everything is delayed.”
> “This is a government announcement with nicer fonts.”
> “They are selling the Policy Address.”

## 3. Audience

### Primary audience

Hong Kong adults roughly aged 25–45 who:

- Follow property, city development, business, technology, or economic news
- Are curious about neighbourhoods, transport, infrastructure, and urban change
- May be renting, considering buying, investing, changing jobs, building a business, or raising a family
- Want to understand Hong Kong without reading long technical documents
- Enjoy maps, comparisons, explainers, and strong local opinions

### Secondary audience

- Young professionals and office workers
- SME owners and managers
- Property-interested readers who dislike agent-style hard selling
- Infrastructure, planning, construction, logistics, and technology professionals
- Hong Kong people interested in the Greater Bay Area
- Overseas Hong Kong readers following city and market changes

### What readers want

Readers want clarity, relevance, and conversation. They do not necessarily want a technical lecture.

They respond to:

- “What this means for you”
- “One map explains it”
- “The real reason behind the headline”
- “Opportunity versus risk”
- “Who can start, supply, or join”
- “District comparison”
- “Before and after”
- “Myth versus reality”
- “What professionals know that most people do not see”

## 4. Coverage Pillars

The publication covers five main pillars. Every article must clearly belong to one primary pillar and may have one secondary pillar.

### 4.1 Property & Living — 樓市與生活

Coverage includes:

- Home prices, rents, transactions, supply, completions, vacancy, and yields
- First-time buyer and renter concerns
- District comparisons
- New developments and redevelopment
- Public housing, subsidised housing, ageing buildings, and urban renewal
- Offices, retail, industrial buildings, logistics property, hotels, and student housing
- Mortgage, interest-rate, affordability, and household-impact stories

Preferred angles:

- “With HK$X, what can you realistically get in these districts?”
- “Why is this district changing?”
- “What happens when a new station, mall, or public facility arrives?”
- “Renting versus buying: what has changed this year?”

### 4.2 City Transformation — 城市大變身

Coverage includes:

- Northern Metropolis
- Kai Tak
- West Kowloon
- New development areas
- Urban renewal
- Land creation and reclamation
- New towns
- District regeneration
- Major public facilities
- Development phasing, land use, and long-term city plans

Preferred angles:

- “Before versus after — what new uses become possible?”
- “What will this area look like in five or ten years?”
- “Which businesses, trades, or services arrive first?”
- “Who can participate — and what still has to be true?”

### 4.3 Infrastructure & Mobility — 基建與交通

Coverage includes:

- MTR projects
- Roads, bridges, tunnels, and highways
- Airport, port, cargo, and logistics systems
- Buses, ferries, cycling, walking, and accessibility
- Cross-boundary transport and GBA connectivity
- Smart mobility, traffic, navigation, and transport technology

Preferred angles:

- “Which districts become more connected — and what opens because of that?”
- “Will this really save time? If yes, for whom?”
- “What can logistics, retail, or construction firms do during the build?”
- “A commuter’s and operator’s guide to a major project”

### 4.4 Economy, Money & Opportunity — 經濟、錢與機會

Coverage includes:

- Hong Kong economic indicators
- Interest rates, inflation, employment, retail sales, tourism, trade, and investment
- Business cycles and sector trends
- Developer finance and investment-property activity
- IPOs, fintech, business formation, consumer trends, and GBA opportunity
- The economic effects of major city projects

Preferred angles:

- “Where is money, talent, or demand flowing?”
- “What does this number change in everyday life or a P&L?”
- “What new market is opening — and what is still thin?”
- “The opportunity and the risk behind the headline”

### 4.5 Technology Changes the City — 科技改變生活

Coverage includes:

- AI and work
- Proptech and smart construction
- Smart city systems
- Fintech and digital government
- CCTV, computer vision, robotics, data platforms, automation, and digital twins
- AI tools that matter to Hong Kong workers and businesses
- Technology’s effect on property, mobility, retail, logistics, and urban life

Preferred angles:

- “Does this actually work in Hong Kong — and who can adopt it?”
- “What problem does it solve?”
- “What new product, service, or workflow becomes possible?”
- “AI hype versus a real deployment a local firm can copy”

## 5. The “Soft Technical” Rule

### The central principle

**Technical depth is the engine room. Entertainment is the showroom.**

The AI researcher and editor must understand the hard material first:

- Planning context
- Engineering constraints
- Traffic and transport implications
- Project phasing
- Land-use implications
- Market data
- Financing and policy context
- Technology limitations
- Regulatory status

But the published story should begin with a human question, not technical jargon.

### Transforming technical topics

| Do not lead with | Lead with |
|---|---|
| “Transport corridor capacity enhancement” | “Will this new route actually make your commute faster?” |
| “Gross floor area and plot ratio controls” | “Why can one site become luxury flats while another becomes public housing?” |
| “Traffic impact assessment methodology” | “How do engineers know whether a new mall will cause gridlock?” |
| “Construction phasing and programme risk” | “Why do major Hong Kong projects take so long?” |
| “Property transaction data” | “Is the market genuinely moving again, or are headlines misleading?” |
| “AI-enabled video analytics” | “Can AI really spot a missing safety helmet on a busy site?” |

### The one-layer-deeper rule

For every mainstream claim, explain one useful layer deeper than a typical social post.

Example:

- Basic claim: “A new railway station is coming.”
- Better explanation: “A station changes travel options, but it does not automatically transform property prices. Timing, walking access, competing supply, jobs, schools, retail, and interest rates still matter.”

### The no-homework rule

If a paragraph reads like it belongs in a planning report, rewrite it.

Use plain language first. Introduce technical terms only when they add genuine precision. When necessary, define them in one sentence.

## 6. Story Formats

The magazine must use recurring formats. Readers should recognise the format before they finish the headline.

### 6.1 一張圖講清楚 — One Visual Explains It

**Purpose:** Fast, highly shareable visual explainer.

Best for:

- Maps
- Timelines
- District comparisons
- Price/rent snapshots
- Transport changes
- “Five things to know” stories

Examples:

- 「一張圖睇晒北部都會區未來交通網」
- 「香港未來 10 年，邊幾區變得最快？」
- 「HK$600 萬買樓：五區選擇一眼睇清」

Structure:

1. Strong visual statement
2. One-sentence answer
3. Three to five key facts
4. “Why it matters”
5. Source box

### 6.2 工程師講人話 — Engineer Explains in Human Language

**Purpose:** Signature authority column.

Best for:

- Infrastructure
- Transport
- Construction
- Planning
- Smart-city technology
- Explaining why projects are complicated

Examples:

- 「工程師講人話：一條新路唔係畫條線就起得」
- 「工程師講人話：點解新商場一開就可能塞車？」
- 「工程師講人話：地盤圍板後面每日究竟做緊咩？」

Structure:

1. Familiar question
2. One surprising fact
3. Simple analogy
4. Three practical reasons or constraints
5. What it means in real life
6. “If you only remember one thing” ending

### 6.3 地圖偵探 — Map Detective

**Purpose:** Turn city development into an exploration story.

Best for:

- New districts
- Development sites
- Station catchments
- Urban renewal
- Road projects
- Retail and lifestyle shifts

Examples:

- 「地圖偵探：啟德點解有咁多位仲未旺？」
- 「地圖偵探：元朗未來 10 年會點變？」

Structure:

1. Map-based hook
2. What is visible now
3. What is planned
4. What is uncertain
5. Who may be affected
6. The next milestone to watch

### 6.4 真相 vs 想像 — Reality vs Hype

**Purpose:** Myth-busting with balance, not cynicism.

Examples:

- 「新站一開，樓價一定升？真相未必咁簡單」
- 「北都會複製深圳？三個相似位，三個根本分別」
- 「減息就等於樓市起飛？仲要睇呢四件事」

Structure:

1. Widely repeated belief
2. Short verdict: true, partly true, misleading, or too early to tell
3. Evidence
4. Counterpoint
5. What readers should watch next

### 6.5 香港人最想知 — The Question Hong Kong Wants Answered

**Purpose:** Practical, search-friendly explainer.

Examples:

- 「北部都會區到底係乜？普通香港人有咩關係？」
- 「買樓前點睇附近地盤、噪音同交通？」
- 「新鐵路幾時先通車？點解工程成日延期？」

Structure:

1. Direct answer in first 80 words
2. Clear definitions
3. What happened
4. Why it matters
5. Timeline / map / comparison
6. FAQ
7. Sources

### 6.6 城市變化週報 — City Change Weekly

**Purpose:** Habit-forming weekly digest for website, Facebook, newsletter, and video.

Structure:

- Three important changes this week
- One map or visual
- One property/economy number
- One infrastructure or technology update
- One “watch this next” item
- One audience poll or discussion question

### 6.7 What They Don’t Tell You — 頭條背後

**Purpose:** Explain the hidden constraint, trade-off, or implementation issue.

Examples:

- 「新樓盤宣傳交通方便，但步行去站原來要幾耐？」
- 「大型基建唔係最難起，最難係點樣同附近生活共存」

Use with care: be fair, evidence-based, and avoid implying wrongdoing without evidence.

### 6.8 一條數睇穿 — One Number Explodes the Headline

One number that rearranges the official story, then the mechanism behind it.

### 6.9 現場筆記 — Fifteen-Minute Place Piece

Even desk research must reconstruct a walkable scene from maps, photos, and primary documents. What is visible now, what is missing, who is stranded.

### 6.10 邊區故事 — District Portrait

CityLab neighbourhood piece: what is visible, what is planned, who is left out.

### 6.11 長題短寫 — Short Visual Essay

400–700 words. The hero diagram does the work.

### 6.12 傳聞與可能性 — Rumour, Kept Open

**Purpose:** Write the city gossip people already share — a new line, a land bid, an AI system, a delayed opening — without closing the door.

Rules:

- Label it **未證實 / 可能性** in the standfirst or a boxed note.
- Separate: what is on record, what people are saying, what would have to be true.
- Do not debunk with a government slogan. Do not confirm with a forum post.
- End with the next fact that would settle it.

### Topic interest test

Do not spend a feature on a story that fails this test. A publishable topic needs **at least two**:

1. A number that changes a household, commute, rent, or a firm’s next step
2. A named district plus a visible change (land, station, rebuild, sale — not only a contract signing)
3. A mechanism most readers do not already know
4. Tension: promised vs delivered, rumour vs record, before vs after
5. A window someone can actually watch (tender date, land sale, opening, data release)
6. Enough sourced body to write without inventing a scene

Kill as a feature (briefing-only or drop):

- Officials restating the Policy Address on the radio
- Department homepage chrome (「了解更多」「我們的願景」「年度計劃」with no list)
- A tender or contract with no dollar, date, or map
- Encoding-garbled nav titles

Daily pack mix, when the harvest allows: one **number or living** story, one **district / map** story, one **tech-or-rumour** story. Never three radio interviews.

### Anti-brochure rule

Official documents are **engine-room evidence**. They are not the voice of the magazine.

- Do not praise policy as 惠民、遠見、成功、穩步推進.
- A tender, contract signing, or Policy Address line is a source — not a compliment.
- If the only copy you can write is a nicer news.gov.hk, kill the draft and find the life, the threat, or the rumour.

## 7. Editorial Style Rules

### Language

- Primary language: **Traditional Chinese**, written naturally for Hong Kong readers.
- Use Cantonese-friendly phrasing in headlines and social posts where appropriate.
- Keep website articles readable in standard written Chinese; do not make every paragraph fully colloquial.
- Use English technical terms where they are common in Hong Kong, but explain them once.
- Use English editions selectively for B2B, international readers, and technical pieces.

### Tone

Use:

- Clear, confident, fair language
- Short sentences mixed with occasional longer explanatory sentences
- Concrete numbers, dates, places, and examples
- A conversational question where it improves flow
- Controlled humour and observation
- Carefully labelled opinion

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon
- Overly formal government language
- Excessive exclamation marks
- Fake urgency
- Unsupported “must buy,” “guaranteed,” “sure win,” or “will definitely rise” claims
- Empty phrases such as “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” or “unprecedented” unless directly supported

### Headline rules

A headline must contain at least one of:

- A familiar Hong Kong place
- A real reader question
- A money, time, or lifestyle consequence
- A tension or myth
- A concrete number or comparison
- A clear “what changed” angle

Good examples:

- 「北都新路網點樣改寫元朗？一張圖睇清未來返工時間」
- 「HK$600 萬買樓：啟德、將軍澳、東涌邊區最啱你？」
- 「新站一開樓價必升？睇返香港三個真實例子」
- 「AI 睇住個地盤，真係可以減少危險？」

Weak examples:

- 「香港基建發展概況」
- 「北部都會區之未來規劃」
- 「人工智能技術在建造業的應用研究」

### Opening rules

The first paragraph must answer:

1. What is opening or being built?
2. Why should a Hong Kong reader — or a local business — care?
3. What will this story help them do or watch next?

Do not begin with a long history lesson or a definition unless the story is explicitly a beginner explainer.

### Anti-boring kill-list

- Do not open with a bureau name, ordinance number, or 「根據 XX 發表」.
- Kill any paragraph that could be pasted onto news.gov.hk unchanged.
- If you can swap the district name and the piece still works, it is too generic — rewrite.
- One perfect analogy, not three.
- Entertainment is craft, not jokes: scale, before/after, a new capability, a beautiful diagram.
- No fake urgency, no 揭秘 / 必看 / 最啱你.
- Every major piece needs **one unforgettable unit**: a number, a map move, a named constraint, or a scene people will repeat in a group chat.
- First 80–120 words: a sourced place, named official, number, or decision — not a fictional shopkeeper. The briefing answers live in the deck, not as the opening lecture.
- Never invent characters, dialogue, streets, or Tuesday-morning scenes. If the source has no person, do not cast one.
- Headlines must be imageable and forward. Prefer 「新站之後：三個社區，三種新局」over consumer-agent lines or dunks.
- Do not lead with delay, missing numbers, or congestion as the only punchline. Those belong in **變數**, after the reader can see what is being built and who can act.

## 8. Article Blueprint

### Standard article structure

Use this magazine-feature spine **in the writing, not as printed headings**. If the reader can see 「現場／機會／機制／誰可以做」the draft has failed.

1. **Headline + unlabeled dek** — never print the word Standfirst
2. **Hero visual** — the share object, or an exact `needs_visual` spec if the asset is not ready
3. **Opening scene** — 80–120 words: a place, a person, something becoming possible
4. **3–4 story-specific section titles** — invented for this piece, like magazine chapters
5. **Opportunity, mechanism, threat, rumour** — woven through scenes (生意 / 投資觀察 / 建造 / 生活 / 威脅 / 未證實傳聞)
6. **A last beat** — the next date, tender, or fact that would settle a rumour
7. **Sources and disclosure**

Forbidden on the page: worksheet headers, （分析）（事實）（預測）, trade bullet lists, press-release cadence.

### Required sidebars

Include one or more when useful:

- **一句講晒 / In one sentence**
- **三個重點 / Three things to know**
- **時間線 / Timeline**
- **地圖 / Map**
- **數字睇真啲 / By the numbers**
- **專業詞解釋 / Term explained**
- **風險提示 / What could go wrong**

### “What it means for you” block

Every relevant story should contain a dedicated block:

> **誰可以做？**

Must cover, when sources allow:

- **生意** — SME, shop, logistics, professional service
- **投資觀察** — what a careful watcher would track (not a buy order)
- **建造** — tender, subcontract, materials, site services

(Older drafts may still say **對你有咩影響？** — rewrite toward these three windows.)

Also useful:

- Renter
- First-time buyer
- Homeowner
- Commuter
- Parent
- SME owner
- Investor / market watcher
- Worker in the affected district

## 9. Research & Evidence Rules

### Source hierarchy

Use this hierarchy when researching and citing claims.

| Tier | Source type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Official government dataset, legislation, statutory notice, gazette, primary company filing, official project document | Main factual evidence |
| 2 | Official company, developer, MTR, government department, listed-company announcement | Project details, timelines, commitments |
| 3 | Established journalism and wire reporting | Context, reporting, interviews, reaction |
| 4 | Consultancy research | Attributed market interpretation and forecast |
| 5 | Social posts, agent commentary, forums, creator content | Sentiment, leads, community reaction only |

### Required verification

Before publication, the AI must:

- Identify the original source of each major factual claim.
- Verify dates, locations, project names, monetary figures, and status.
- Distinguish proposed, approved, funded, tendered, under construction, completed, and operational stages.
- Separate fact, analysis, forecast, and opinion.
- Record the source URL and retrieval date.
- Flag uncertainty rather than inventing confidence.

### Preferred primary sources

Prioritise:

- Hong Kong Government Information Services
- GovHK News
- Policy Address and Budget documents
- DATA.GOV.HK
- Rating and Valuation Department
- Land Registry
- Census and Statistics Department
- Hong Kong Monetary Authority
- Development Bureau
- Planning Department
- Town Planning Board
- Lands Department
- CEDD
- Highways Department
- Transport Department
- Transport and Logistics Bureau
- MTR Corporation
- Airport Authority
- Northern Metropolis official website
- CIC and CITF
- Cyberport, HKSTP, HKPC, InvestHK
- Marine Department and Hong Kong Maritime and Port Development Board
- Official announcements from listed companies, developers, banks, and technology vendors

### Handling consultancy reports

Consultancy reports are valuable but are not neutral public facts.

Correct framing:

> “According to CBRE’s latest market report…”

Incorrect framing:

> “The market will definitely rise…”

Where material, compare more than one view and cross-check with official data.

### Search and AI rules

AI may assist with discovery, extraction, tagging, summarisation, outlining, and draft writing. It must not:

- Invent sources or citations
- Quote text not found in a source
- Treat a search-result snippet as complete evidence
- Present unverified social-media claims as facts
- Copy long protected text from a paid article
- Publish automatically without human editorial approval

## 10. Financial and Legal Boundaries

### Investment and property disclaimer

For any story involving property, financial markets, investment themes, mortgage rates, or price expectations, include an appropriate version of:

> 本文只作新聞、教育及一般資訊用途，不構成投資、置業、法律、按揭或財務建議。讀者應按自身情況尋求合資格專業人士意見。

### Editorial safety rules

Do not:

- Tell readers to buy, sell, rent, invest in, or avoid a specific property, stock, fund, or financial product.
- Promise price gains, rental returns, or investment results.
- Make defamatory claims or imply misconduct without strong documented evidence and editorial/legal review.
- Use leaked personal data, doxxing, or private information.
- Treat developer marketing material as independent evidence.
- Misrepresent concept images, renderings, or AI images as site photographs or confirmed project designs.

### Use careful language

Prefer:

- “值得留意”
- “可能受惠”
- “市場正在反映”
- “仍有不確定性”
- “取決於”
- “需要觀察”
- “不代表必然結果”

Avoid:

- “必升”
- “穩賺”
- “最後上車機會”
- “保證回報”
- “一定會”

## 11. Visual and Social Style

### Visual purpose

Visuals must explain something. They are not decoration.

Preferred visual assets:

- Maps
- Timelines
- District comparison cards
- Before/after comparisons
- Simple charts
- Annotated aerial imagery
- Real site photographs with location/date context
- Project diagrams from public documents, clearly attributed
- Short vertical video explainers

### Image integrity

- Never use AI-generated images as evidence of a real place, real construction progress, real project design, or real event.
- Clearly label AI concept art, illustrative visualisations, and reconstructed diagrams.
- Attribute government maps, developer renderings, report figures, and third-party images.
- Use real maps and primary documents whenever the location/design/status matters.

### Social adaptation

For each major article, create:

- One Facebook link post with a clear opinion/question
- One Instagram carousel or short Reel
- One Threads-style conversational take
- One newsletter snippet
- Optional Xiaohongshu or YouTube short adaptation where appropriate

The social post should tease the article, not reproduce the entire article.

Facebook and web teasers are a **pull-quote + visual thesis**, not 「你覺得邊區好？」. Invite a precise comment, for example: 「如果新站救唔到個區，仲有邊樣先救到？」

## 12. Content Balance

### Target editorial mix

For every 50 published pieces, aim for approximately:

| Content type | Target share | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Visual and shareable city stories | 35% | Maps, one-number, district portraits |
| Narrative explainers | 25% | New feature spine; trust and search |
| Property/economy with a character and a chart | 15% | Timely relevance |
| Myth / tension / 頭條背後 | 15% | Brand personality |
| Deep technical prestige longread | 10% | Monthly “we went deeper than anyone” |

### Publishing rhythm

Suggested lean operation:

- 2–3 short news/visual posts each week
- 1 substantial explainer or analysis each week
- 1 weekly city-change digest
- 1 signature column every two weeks
- 1 original field/case-study piece each month

Quality is more important than volume. Do not publish thin AI-generated articles merely to appear active.

## 13. AI Research Workflow

### Step 1: Discover

Collect candidates from:

- Official press rooms
- Government datasets
- Planning and gazette notices
- MTR / developer / listed-company announcements
- Consultancy reports
- Reputable local and international news
- Official technology vendor newsrooms

### Step 2: Classify

For each candidate, assign:

- Primary content pillar
- Geographic area / district
- Story type
- Reader impact: low / medium / high
- Entertainment potential: low / medium / high
- Authority score: 1–5
- Timeliness: breaking / current / evergreen
- Publication recommendation: publish / monitor / discard

### Step 3: Verify

Confirm:

- Original source
- Date
- Status
- Amounts and units
- Place names
- Project scope
- Whether the story is a proposal, decision, announcement, tender, construction activity, or completed outcome

### Step 4: Find the human angle

Ask:

- Who cares?
- What changes in their life?
- What question are people already asking?
- What is surprising?
- What is the trade-off?
- Is there a map, number, time-saving, price difference, or before/after visual?

### Step 5: Draft

Write the article using the standard article blueprint.

### Step 6: Editorial review

A human editor must approve:

- Accuracy
- Tone
- Source links
- Financial/legal wording
- Visual labels
- Headline fairness
- Claims about future outcomes

### Step 7: Repurpose

Convert approved article into social posts and newsletter material.

## 14. Quality Checklist

Before publishing, the AI or editor must answer “yes” to all applicable questions.

### Accuracy

- Is every major claim supported by a source?
- Are dates, numbers, names, and locations correct?
- Did we state whether a project is proposed, approved, under construction, or completed?
- Did we distinguish fact from analysis and forecast?

### Reader value

- Does the first paragraph explain why this matters?
- Can a non-specialist understand it?
- Does it answer a real question?
- Does it include one useful deeper insight?
- Does the reader leave with a 生意、投資觀察、or 建造 window — not only a complaint, and not a policy slogan?
- Does it explain uncertainty or trade-offs where relevant?

### Tone

- Is it intelligent but not academic?
- Is it entertaining without being sensational?
- Is it constructive without being a press release?
- Is it confident but not overconfident?
- Is it locally specific to Hong Kong?
- Did we remove unnecessary jargon?

### Visuals

- Does each visual explain something?
- Are maps, images, diagrams, and renderings accurately labelled?
- Are AI-generated visuals clearly marked as illustrations?

### Compliance

- Is any investment/property disclaimer needed?
- Did we avoid direct personal investment or property advice?
- Did we avoid unsupported allegations or misleading certainty?

## 15. Example Editorial Prompts

### Prompt: news brief

```text
Write a Traditional Chinese Hong Kong city-magazine feature (prestige visual voice).

Topic: [TOPIC]
Primary sources: [URLS] — cite only these URLs. Do not invent sources.

Requirements:
- Open with 80–120 words: a concrete place, a person or role, and what is becoming possible. Never start with 根據 XX 發表.
- One unforgettable unit (number, new capability, or scene).
- Layer B mechanism in plain language, one analogy.
- 機會 must include 生意 + 投資觀察 + 建造 when sources allow. Map the landscape; never tell anyone to buy or sell.
- 誰可以做？ as concrete lives or firms. Also: how tech might change a Tuesday, and the threat.
- If a rumour is in the air, write it as 未證實可能性 — keep the door open.
- Label fact vs analysis vs forecast vs rumour.
- Do not promote government policy. Official quotes are claims.
- Do not dunk on the city. Do not sound like news.gov.hk.
```

### Prompt: soft technical explainer

```text
Write a 1,200-word Traditional Chinese explainer in “工程師講人話” (Works in Progress craft, not YouTube explainer).

Topic: [TOPIC]
Audience: Hong Kong general readers aged 25–45.

Requirements:
- Open with a scene of change, not jargon and not 根據 XX 發表.
- Explain the technical mechanism accurately, using exactly one analogy.
- One unforgettable unit.
- Include “誰可以做？” covering 生意、投資觀察、建造 when sources allow.
- Include how tech might improve a life, and the threat.
- Include risks, uncertainty, and limitations after the opportunity is clear.
- Cite only provided primary sources. Distinguish facts from opinions.
```

### Prompt: property/economy analysis

```text
Write an accessible Hong Kong property/economy analysis based only on the sources provided.

Topic: [TOPIC]
Sources: [URLS]

Requirements:
- Open with a household- or firm-scale scene of change, then the number that opens a decision.

```

### Prompt: social carousel

Turn this article into an 8-slide Instagram/Facebook carousel in Traditional Chinese.

## 16. Final Editorial Instruction

When in doubt, choose the version of a story that is:

- More accurate rather than more dramatic
- More locally relevant rather than more generic
- More explanatory rather than more jargon-heavy
- More visual rather than more verbose
- More human rather than more bureaucratic
- More honest about uncertainty rather than more certain
- More useful to ordinary Hong Kong readers rather than more impressive to specialists

The goal is not simply to report that Hong Kong is changing.

The goal is to help people **see how tech might change a life, map 生意／投資觀察／建造 windows, name the threat, and keep rumours open** — without selling government policy.
